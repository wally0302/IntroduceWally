# 8. How I Work

## Principle 1 — Understand before saying no

Story:

Previously, urgent stakeholder requests could trigger an immediate execution-focused response:

- too urgent
- too expensive
- engineering may need overtime
- existing schedule will be disrupted

Current approach:

- first accept the request as information
- ask why the change is needed
- understand the desired outcome
- separate the stakeholder's proposed solution from the actual need
- search for alternative ways to satisfy the same motivation

Key insight:

> The requested implementation is not always the real need.

---

## Principle 2 — Bring options, not just problems

Story:

Earlier proposals often contained only one solution.

When the supervisor / stakeholder rejected it, the discussion had to restart.

Current approach:

- prepare A / B / multiple options
- compare pros and cons
- explain cost and risk
- state preferred option
- explain why that option is recommended

Memorable phrase:

> 讓主管做選擇題，而不是問答題。

Public English interpretation:

> Turn open-ended problems into decision-ready options.

---

## Principle 3 — Make trade-offs visible

Current approach:

When a request cannot be solved through a simpler alternative:

- explain implementation cost
- explain time impact
- explain product / UX impact
- explain engineering constraint
- let stakeholders understand what is being traded off
- confirm direction with shared context

---

## Principle 4 — Own the outcome

Growth:

Wally used to feel more like a messenger between teams.

Current understanding:

The PM should:

- translate vague requests
- explain why the work matters
- help the team understand why a direction was chosen
- keep work moving
- own follow-up after the decision
- respond when reality changes

Key idea:

> PM is not just passing information between departments.

---

## Principle 5 — Use AI as leverage

Current use / direction:

AI can help:

- organize information
- prepare proposals
- generate alternative solutions
- summarize meetings
- research faster
- prototype faster
- automate repetitive PM tasks

But:

> AI can generate options. The PM still owns the judgment.
